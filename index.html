Investor awareness

Common myths about mutual funds

Myth "You need lakhs to start investing."

Fact You can start a SIP with as little as ₹500 a month.

Myth "Mutual funds are unregulated."

Fact All mutual funds in India are regulated by SEBI. Your units are held in your own name with the fund house.

Myth "My money will be locked in."

Fact Most open-ended funds can be redeemed on any business day. Only ELSS has a 3-year lock-in. Some funds have an exit load.

Myth "I must time the market."

Fact A SIP buys more units when prices are low and fewer when high. Staying invested for longer matters more than timing.

Mutual funds, made simple

Short, plain-language guides. Pick a topic.

What is a mutual fund?

A mutual fund pools money from many investors. A professional fund manager invests it in shares, bonds or both, as per the scheme's stated goal.

  • You get units for your money. The price of one unit is the NAV, published every business day.
  • Your units are held in your own name with the fund house, not with the distributor.
  • Every mutual fund in India is regulated by SEBI.
  • You can start with as little as ₹500 a month in many schemes.

SIP vs lumpsum

SIP

A fixed amount every month, auto-debited from your bank.

  • Good for monthly income earners
  • Buys more units when prices are low
  • Builds a saving habit

Lumpsum

A large amount invested in one go.

  • Good for a bonus, maturity or sale proceeds
  • The full amount is exposed to the market at once
  • Can be spread out using an STP

Types of funds

Equity

Invest mainly in company shares. Higher growth potential, more ups and downs. Best for 5+ years.

Debt

Invest in bonds and money-market papers. Steadier, for short to medium goals.

Hybrid

A mix of equity and debt in one fund. A middle path.

ELSS (tax saving)

Equity funds with a 3-year lock-in. Eligible for deduction under Section 80C in the old tax regime.

Index funds & ETFs

Follow a market index like the Nifty 50. Usually lower cost.

Liquid funds

Very short-term debt funds. Often used to park an emergency fund.

Reading the riskometer

Every scheme shows a SEBI riskometer with one of six levels. Check it before you invest, and make sure it matches your comfort with risk.

LowLow to ModerateModerateModerately HighHighVery High

Most equity funds sit at "Very High". That does not mean "bad". It means the value can go up and down a lot in the short term.

KYC & documents checklist

KYC (Know Your Customer) is a one-time check. Once done, it works across all fund houses.

  • PAN card
  • Aadhaar (for address and e-KYC)
  • Bank account details (cancelled cheque or statement)
  • A recent photo
  • Mobile number and email in your own name
  • Nominee details

Tip: Always add a nominee. It makes it much easier for your family to claim your investments.

Glossary – jargon in plain words

NAV
Net Asset Value: the price of one unit of a fund, published every business day.
AUM
Assets Under Management: the total money a fund (or distributor) manages.
Expense ratio
The yearly cost of running a fund, as a % of your investment. It is already taken out of the NAV.
Exit load
A small fee some funds charge if you withdraw before a set period, for example within 1 year.
Lock-in period
A time during which you cannot withdraw. ELSS has a 3-year lock-in.
CAGR
The average yearly growth rate of a one-time investment over a period.
XIRR
The yearly return when you invest at different times, as in a SIP.
Folio number
Your account number with a fund house.
CAS
Consolidated Account Statement: one statement showing all your mutual fund holdings across fund houses.
SIP
Systematic Investment Plan: a fixed amount invested automatically every month.
STP
Systematic Transfer Plan: moves money from one fund to another in the same fund house, in regular parts.
SWP
Systematic Withdrawal Plan: a fixed amount paid out to you regularly from your investment.
ELSS
Equity Linked Savings Scheme: a tax-saving equity fund with a 3-year lock-in.
Benchmark
A market index a fund compares its performance against, such as the Nifty 50.
Growth option
Gains stay invested in the fund and the NAV grows.
IDCW
Income Distribution cum Capital Withdrawal: the fund pays out part of the value. The payout comes from your own investment.
Direct plan
Bought straight from the fund house, with no distributor. Lower expense ratio.
Regular plan
Bought through a distributor. The expense ratio includes the distributor's commission.
Redemption
Selling your units to take money out. It is usually credited to your bank in 1–3 business days.
NFO
New Fund Offer: the launch period of a new scheme.

FAQ

Questions investors often ask

Why invest through a mutual fund distributor?

A distributor helps you choose suitable schemes, completes KYC and paperwork, sets up SIPs, and reviews your portfolio with you every year. Having someone to talk to also helps you stay calm when markets fall.

Do you charge a fee? How are you paid?

I do not charge you any fee. As a mutual fund distributor, I receive commission from the fund houses (AMCs) on investments in regular plans. This commission is part of the scheme's expense ratio. Details of the commission I receive are available on request.

Is my money safe? Where does it go?

Mutual funds in India are regulated by SEBI. Your money goes straight from your bank account to the fund house, and units are held in your name. I never handle your money. Note that the value of your investment can go up or down with the market.

What is the minimum amount to start a SIP?

Many schemes accept SIPs from ₹500 or ₹1,000 a month. You can increase, reduce, pause or stop a SIP later.

How do I withdraw my money?

You can redeem online. For most open-ended funds, money reaches your registered bank account within 1–3 working days. ELSS has a 3-year lock-in, and some schemes charge an exit load if you redeem early.

Are you a SEBI-registered investment adviser?

No. I am an AMFI-registered Mutual Fund Distributor (ARN-185973). I help you invest in mutual fund schemes and earn commission from fund houses. I do not charge advisory fees.
